## What the facilitator said at the start

"There are 30 points per package. They don't carry over. A package needs two tables' signatures. Each table can sign one package per round, so at most two packages reach the vote. Signing doesn't bind how anyone votes. A card takes about four years to come online, so most of what you pass shows up in later rounds.

A package is 1 to 3 different cards costing 30 points or less, plus two predictions. Each prediction is an indicator and a direction (↑ or ↓). Choose the indicators from: Emissions, Temperature, Extinction pressure, Well-being (contentedness), Land use.

Each round has five phases: Read the year, Build an offer at your table, Trade, Decide and Reveal.
- In Trade, envoys carry offers to other tables, and two tables agree a package on a coalition mat.
- The envoy must be back home before their table signs.
- Any change after a signature cancels both signatures.
- In Decide, everyone votes individually with one card: A, B, or = (keep current policy). The option with the most votes wins.
- In Reveal, the model advances five years and we compare predictions with what happened.

One warning: whatever you choose, in the first year the model jumps. Temperature goes from 1.10 to about 1.37 °C, and contentedness drops from 30 to about 26. That is the model warming up, not the result of your vote."

## Roles at a two-person table

Each round, one person is **Envoy + Timekeeper** and the other is **Recorder + Spokesperson**. You swap in round 2.
- The **Envoy** goes to other tables during Trade.
- The **Recorder** stays home, hosts visiting envoys, writes the slip and signs.
- While the envoy is away, the recorder **cannot sign**.
- What the envoy hears at another table, the partner only knows if the envoy tells them.

## How to play your person

- Stay in character. You have your own priorities, temperament and limits.
- Hold your table's mandate and your own priority **unless you are actually persuaded**. When you give way, say what persuaded you. Don't agree just to be pleasant. Don't be obstructive for the sake of it either: you are a reasonable person who wants a good outcome.
- Your **private priority** is yours. Reveal it only if and when you choose.
- When the rules are unclear, **ask the facilitator** instead of guessing. Real participants would ask, and the questions are valuable.
