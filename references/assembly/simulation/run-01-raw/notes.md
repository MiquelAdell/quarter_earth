# Orchestrator notes for observer

- Harness: Expand Nature Preserves pushes land use from ~63% to ~99% when it comes online (protected land counts in land use). Players may read it as a disaster; the Report should say protected land is included.
- Deck A card text prints the coal conflict as "Cannot combine with", which reads like a hard rule, but the run sheet treats it as a warning only.
- Opening: 6 of 7 people asked how a promise is judged (final value vs whole path; the year-one jump; changing it afterwards). The run sheet only says "one indicator, a direction, a target and a year". The facilitator needs a scripted answer.
- Prediction slip and wall timeline are laid out for the 6-round format. Carme labelled her 2032 slip "round 5", Jordi wrote "end of round 1 (2032)". For a 2-round rehearsal, the year/round mapping confuses people.
- Núria named partner tables "Society" and "Land & nature", which are the card theme labels, not the table names (Living Standards / Living World). The theme labels on cards and the table names don't match.
- R1 Build: Solar Push appears in 4 of 7 proposals (Marta, Pau, Jordi, Carme, Àlex). Every table wants the one card that is pure substitution. Likely convergence: low conflict, the "everybody agrees" risk.
- Both EI partners conceded to each other's number at the same moment (Marta → 48, Pau → 45). With simultaneous turns this is an artefact of the simulation, but a real-world analogue exists.
- Carme asked whether two tables' packages can both contain Solar Push. The run sheet doesn't say what happens when both mats carry the same card and the first one wins.
- The promise drove card choice: LS locked a well-being promise ("above 30"), and that made the degrowth activist give up Degrowth for Luxury for All, because "it's the only card that clearly raises contentedness". The promise, written before any cards were discussed, acted as a binding constraint. Worth discussing: is that intended?
- Misrouted visit: Pau sent Marta to "Land & Nature" (meaning LW); she couldn't map it to a table code and went to FL. The theme labels cost a visit slot. In slot 1, two tables got both envoys and two got none.
- Promises as constraints, again: FL's land promise (below 57%) made Jordi refuse Expand Nature Preserves; LW's whole strategy hinges on Preserves. The mandate/promise clash makes for real negotiation.
- R1 outcome of Trade: 1 mat (FL+EI). LS and LW, the two tables with the most polarised packages (Luxury vs Preserves), were both left out. With two mats and four tables, the two "leftover" tables can only pair with each other, and their packages were incompatible. Structural: one round can freeze out half the room.
- Signing protocol in the sim: the recorder's "will sign if identical" plus a matching counterpart meant signed. Núria (FL envoy) had recommended not signing until confirmed and lost her private priority (Organic). The decision moved to the recorder without a second conversation. Check whether the real table would sign that fast.
- Oriol, the "quiet" persona, wrote long, decisive replies, so the sim doesn't model quiet participants well. "Written contributions count" can't really be tested here.
- R1 Reveal: the cards came online in 2026 and moved the numbers hard in one year. Land use fell 58.7% → 36.4% (Vegetarian Mandate) and extinction 90.2 → 64.0. As a result FL's promise (land below 57% by 2032) and LW's (extinction below 88) were already met after round 1, and LS's (well-being above 30) looks far away at 21.6. These single-year step changes make 2032 promises nearly decided by round 1. Expected size of effects isn't on the cards ("Three-quarters of animal-calorie demand moves..." gives no sense of scale).
- Emissions rose 2024–25 (52.9, 54.3) before the drop, so the year-by-year view shows things getting worse before the cards land.
- R2: Ban Outdoor Cats appears in 6 of 6 proposals so far. The cheap 5-point card is universal filler, which is what Oriol's persona predicted.
- R2: Luxury for All went from refused by everyone (R1) to proposed by 4 people (Jordi, Carme, Laia, plus LS). The well-being drop to 21.6, and the visible link between Vegetarian Mandate and the contentedness cost, created a coalition for it. The Reveal changed the negotiation.
- Coal conflict was caught from the card text alone by Pau and Laia: "Solar Push already used that coal". The printed "Cannot combine with" line works.
- Recorder deference: in R2 all four recorders adopted the envoy's proposal ("you've persuaded me"). In R1 the recorder sided with the envoy at FL and LS. Inside pairs the agents are very deferential: 6 of 8 Build exchanges ended in the recorder taking the partner's package. Likely an LLM agreeableness artefact; humans would hold out more.
- Luxury flip-flop: Carme and Laia dropped Luxury in the same turn that Núria accepted it. Ban Outdoor Cats is in all 4 slips. 4 tables, 4 slips, Cats everywhere, so it could end up on both mats (the run sheet doesn't say whether the same card may appear on both mats).
- Phase Out Coal as filler: two LW players proposed spending 10 points on Phase Out Coal *because* it does nothing. Points expire anyway, so a known no-op card is a free fill. The coal conflict turns into a degenerate move rather than a trap.
- R2: Degrowth is on both mats, so whichever wins, Degrowth passes. The vote is really only about Organic+Cats vs Preserves. The run sheet doesn't mention the same card on both mats. The room may not realise the shared card makes the vote narrower than it looks.
- R2 vote: 4–4 tie, pure sponsor-bloc voting. When all four tables sponsor (2 mats × 2 tables) and pairs vote together, a tie is the natural outcome. The second-tie rule then advances current policy even though everyone voted for Degrowth. Big structural finding: an even number of equally sized tables plus "everyone signs one mat" produces deadlocks.
- OBSERVER-ONLY COUNTERFACTUALS (engine, same 2022 history; 2027 decision varied), 2032 values:
  - nothing passed: Emissions 52.71, Extinction 69.3, Well-being 19.7, Land 38.8%
  - Degrowth only: identical to nothing (52.71 / 69.3 / 19.7 / 38.8%). Degrowth has NO visible effect within one year.
  - Package B (Preserves + Degrowth): 52.71 / 69.3 / 19.7 / **68.8%** land. FL's promise would have broken, with no extinction gain by 2032.
  - Package A (actual): 52.73 / **61.9** / 19.7 / 40.4%. The extinction drop (−7.4 vs nothing) comes from Ban Outdoor Cats; Organic adds +1.6 pts land.
  So the emissions rise (+5.9) is baseline growth. No R2 card affects emissions by 2032, and both packages' "Emissions ↓" forecasts were doomed by the one-year lag.
- CARD TEXT vs EFFECT: Ban Outdoor Cats says "Lowers extinction pressure by 0.5 on the biodiversity index (opening value 90.2)", but its measured effect is about −7.4 on the reported index within one year. Players (Oriol: "is 0.5 worth less now?") judged it negligible because of the card text. Card copy misleads by roughly 15×.
- Round-2 cards only get one year of effect before the 2032 end. In a 2-round rehearsal round 2 is mostly theatre: the Reveal can't show what was chosen, except instant-effect cards (Cats). In the 6-round format this only bites in the last round.
- Misattribution in R2: several players read the emissions rise as Degrowth failing or even raising emissions. The engine shows Degrowth had zero effect by 2032 and the rise is the baseline trend. Without a "what if nothing passed" line on the Report, players credit or blame the last cards passed for background drift. Strongest argument for showing a baseline or delta-vs-no-action column on the Report.
