# Assembly glossary (en / es / ca)

Every recurring term in the assembly kit, the facilitator guide and the operator screens. `strings.csv` and the translated run sheets use these terms. If you change a term here, change it in both places in the same commit.

Spanish is Spain Spanish. Catalan is central Catalan. Neither has had a fluent review yet; see the outstanding terms in `README.md`.

Column notes:

- **es.csv** means the Spanish term was reused from `game/translations/es.csv`, so paper and screen match.
- **⚠** means the Catalan choice (or both translations) is tentative and needs a fluent reviewer.

## Register

| Who is addressed | en | es | ca |
|---|---|---|---|
| A table (mandate sheets, mats, slips, prompts said aloud) | you (plural) | *vosotros*, plural imperative (*escribid*, *firmad*) | *vosaltres* (*escriviu*, *signeu*) |
| The facilitator (run sheet) | you | *tú* (*dilo*, *reparte*) | *tu* (*digues-ho*, *reparteix*) |
| Print instructions for whoever prints and cuts the kit | imperative | infinitive (*Cortar por…*, *imprimir 4 copias*) | infinitive (*Retallar per…*, *imprimir 4 còpies*) |
| Operator messages | neutral | impersonal (*Hay que quitar…*) | impersonal (*Cal treure…*) |

Gender: roles use function nouns or epicene nouns (*enlace*, *portavoz*, *persona facilitadora*), never *-o/-a* pairs. A table is *mesa* / *taula* (feminine), so sponsoring tables are *mesas promotoras* / *taules promotores*. A card is *carta* (feminine), so card-level words agree in the feminine (*Sustituida*, *ninguna*, *la segunda no hace nada*).

## Numbers and units

Values that come from the model are never typed into a translation. They are placeholders (`%{value}`) with a `params` entry, which names the W1 derivation and the format. `check.py` computes the value from the fixtures and renders it per locale.

| Format | en | es | ca |
|---|---|---|---|
| decimal (`dec1`, `dec2`) | 51.6 · 1.10 | 51,6 · 1,10 | 51,6 · 1,10 |
| percent (`pct`) | 35% | 35 % (non-breaking space) | 35 % (non-breaking space) |
| integer (`int`) | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| thousands | 12,500 | 12 500 (none for 4 digits: 7950) | 12.500 |
| years | 2052 (never separated) | 2052 | 2052 |
| sign | +1.10 °C | +1,10 °C | +1,10 °C |
| temperature | 1.10 °C | 1,10 °C | 1,10 °C |
| emissions | 51.6 Gt CO₂-eq per year | 51,6 Gt CO₂-eq al año | 51,6 Gt CO₂-eq l’any |
| energy | 26.0 PWh per year | 26,0 PWh al año | 26,0 PWh l’any |
| large counts | 7.95 billion | 7,95 mil millones | 7,95 mil milions |

Rules:

- **Spaces before units.** Put a space between a number and its unit (°C, %, Gt, PWh) in es and ca. Renderers (W6/W7) should make every space before a unit non-breaking, so a value never wraps away from its unit. `check.py` already inserts U+00A0 before % for es and ca. English keeps "35%" with no space.
- **"Billion" is a false friend.** *Billón* / *bilió* is 10¹². English "billion" is *mil millones* / *mil milions*.
- **Keep unit symbols the same in every language.** Use CO₂ with a subscript 2, and °C, Gt and PWh as they are. Only the word around the symbol is translated ("per year", *al año*, *l’any*).
- **Arrows (→, ↑, ↓) are language-neutral.** Always print them next to a word as well.
- **Integers that are rules, not data, may stay literal.** Examples are 30 points, 4 years, 1–3 cards and round numbers. `check.py` still requires every number to agree across en, es and ca.
- **Write fragment-free phrases.** Where a template still assembles a sentence from fragments (see README gaps), the es/ca fragments are chosen so the assembled sentence still reads naturally.
